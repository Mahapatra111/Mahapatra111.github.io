American Mahjong glossary

Game night has its own vocabulary. Here are the words you’ll hear most, explained in plain English, in alphabetical order. Bookmark this page and keep it handy for your first few games.

Terms

Bams, Craks and Dots
The three suits, each numbered 1 to 9, with four copies of every tile.
Blind pass
On the last pass of each Charleston, passing on some of the tiles coming to you without looking at them. Also called stealing. ⚠ check with your group
Calling
Asking for the tile just discarded, to finish an exposed group of three or more or to declare Mahjong.
Card
The yearly list of winning hands everyone at the table plays from.
Charleston
The passing of tiles before play begins: right, across, left, and an optional second round.
Concealed (C)
A line that must stay on your rack; only the winning tile may come from a discard.
Consecutive run
Numbers in a row, such as 3 4 5 6, starting anywhere they fit.
Courtesy pass
An optional final swap of up to three tiles with the player across. ⚠ check with your group
Dead hand
A hand that can no longer win this round, for example after an exposure that fits no line or with the wrong number of tiles. The player stops drawing and calling but still pays the winner. It happens to everyone now and then. ⚠ check with your group
Dealer (East)
The player who starts with 14 tiles and makes the first discard. The role moves on after every hand.
Discard
The tile you put out at the end of your turn, naming it aloud.
Dragons
Red, Green and White, four of each. Red goes with Craks, Green with Bams, White with Dots.
Exposed (X)
A line you may build with groups placed face-up after calling discards.
Exposure
A group placed face-up on the table after calling a discard. It must fit the line you’re making.
Flowers
Eight tiles that all count as identical. Many lines use them.
Joker
One of eight wild tiles, usable only in groups of three or more. ⚠ check with your group
Joker exchange
On your turn, taking a joker from an exposed group by giving the real tile it stands for. ⚠ check with your group
Jokerless
A winning hand with no jokers; its value usually doubles.
Kong
Four identical tiles.
Like numbers
The same number in different suits.
Mahjong
Declaring a win: your 14 tiles exactly match one line of the card.
Mahjong in error
Declaring Mahjong by mistake. If no one else has shown their tiles because of it, the player can usually fix it and keep playing; groups handle penalties differently. ⚠ check with your group
Matching dragon
The dragon that goes with a suit.
Opposite dragon
A dragon that doesn’t go with that suit.
Pair
Two identical tiles. Jokers can’t be used in pairs.
Pung
Three identical tiles.
Quint
Five identical tiles; needs jokers unless they’re flowers.
Self-pick
Winning with a tile you drew from the wall yourself. Everyone pays double. ⚠ check with your group
Sextet
Six identical tiles.
Single
One tile on its own. Jokers can’t be singles.
Soap
Nickname for the White Dragon. In years like 2 0 2 6, it stands for zero.
Wall
The rows of face-down tiles that players draw from.
Wall game
A hand that ends with no winner because the wall ran out. Nobody pays.
Winds
North, East, West and South, four of each.
Group
A set of identical tiles in a hand: a single, pair, pung, kong, quint or sextet.
Hand
Either your 14 winning tiles or one round of play, from the deal to a win or a wall game.
House rules
The choices your group makes where play varies, such as extra payments or how to play short-handed. Agree on them before you start.
Line
One winning hand printed on the card, with its point value and an X or C.
Point value
The number printed beside a line: the starting value of the hand when someone wins with it.
Rack
The stand in front of you that holds your tiles. It also means the tiles on it.
Section
A group of lines on the card that share one idea, such as even numbers or a year.
Stray
A tile that fits none of the lines you’re considering; the first candidate to pass or discard.

A few phrases you’ll hear at the table

  • “Same color, same suit.” A reminder of how to read the card’s colors: groups printed in the same color must be the same suit.
  • “I’m calling.” Someone wants the tile that was just discarded.
  • “Is that jokerless?” Asked after a win, because it doubles the points.
  • “Who’s East?” Who deals this hand.
  • “Mahj!” Short for Mahjong, said when declaring a win.

Words beginners often mix up

  • Pung and pair. A pung is three tiles; a pair is two. Jokers can go in a pung but never in a pair.
  • Exposed and concealed. These describe both groups (face-up or on your rack) and lines on the card (X or C).
  • Matching and opposite dragons. Matching goes with the suit; opposite doesn’t. The card’s note tells you which.
  • Self-pick and calling. Self-pick means you drew the winning tile; calling means you took a discard.

Why words matter

Knowing the vocabulary makes it much easier to follow explanations, ask questions, and spot mistakes kindly. When a teacher says “you can’t use a joker in a pair” or “that line is concealed”, you’ll know exactly what they mean.
