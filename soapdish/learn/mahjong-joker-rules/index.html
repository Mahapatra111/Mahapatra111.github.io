Mahjong joker rules for beginners

Jokers are the most powerful tiles in American Mahjong, and the most misunderstood. Learn the handful of rules below and you’ll use them confidently, spot when someone else uses them wrongly, and know when to swap one out of an exposed group.

How many jokers are there?

A set has 8 jokers, alongside the suits, winds, dragons and flowers that make up 152 tiles in total. With only eight to go around, picking up even one or two changes how quickly you can finish.

Rule 1: only in groups of three or more

A joker can stand in for any tile in a group of three or more identical tiles: a pung, kong, quint or sextet ⚠ check with your group. That includes groups on your rack and groups you’ve exposed on the table. So if you need four 7 Dots and have three, a joker can be the fourth. If you have one 7 Dot and three jokers, that works too.

Groups don’t even need a real tile in them: a group can be made entirely of jokers, as long as it is a pung or larger ⚠ check with your group. Three jokers can be a pung of 5 Bams, for example.

Rule 2: never in singles or pairs

This is the rule beginners break most. A joker can never be used as a single tile or as part of a pair. If a line calls for a pair of North winds, you need two real North winds.

It goes further: jokers can’t be used anywhere in a part of a hand made of single tiles, such as a year spelled out tile by tile, a run of singles like 1 2 3 4 5, or the four winds as singles. Each of those tiles must be real.

Why quints and sextets need jokers

There are only four copies of each number, wind and dragon tile. So a quint (five) or sextet (six) of them always needs at least one or two jokers. Flowers are the exception: there are eight, so a group of five or six flowers can be all real.

Calling a discard with jokers

You may take another player’s discard to finish a group of three or more, then place that group face-up. A discard can only be called to complete an exposed group of three or more, or to declare Mahjong, and you expose the group right away, using jokers if needed. For example, holding one 9 Crak and a joker, you can call a discarded 9 Crak and expose 9C 9C joker as a pung.

Swapping a joker out (redemption)

Jokers sitting in exposed groups are up for grabs. On your turn, you may take a joker from any player’s exposed group by giving them the real tile it stands for, from your rack or the tile you just drew ⚠ check with your group. If a neighbor has exposed 6 Dots, 6 Dots and a joker, and you hold a 6 Dot, you can swap your 6 Dot for their joker.

A few points to remember:

  • Do it on your own turn.
  • The tile you give must be exactly the tile the joker represents.
  • You can’t take a discard just to swap it for a joker, and a discarded joker generally can’t be picked up ⚠ check with your group.

Jokers are never passed

During the Charleston and the courtesy pass, jokers are never passed. You keep every joker you’re dealt or receive. See the Charleston explained for the full passing sequence.

Jokerless hands score more

A winning hand that uses no jokers at all is called jokerless, and its value usually doubles. There’s one exception: hands that can’t use jokers anyway, such as hands made only of singles and pairs, don’t get the jokerless bonus ⚠ check with your group. Our guide to scoring at game night shows how the doubling works.

Strategy: when to use a joker

  • Use jokers to finish groups you can’t easily complete. A kong of a tile that’s already been discarded twice is hard to build with real tiles.
  • Keep an eye on exposed jokers. Every joker another player exposes is a joker you might win back with the right tile.
  • Weigh the jokerless bonus. If you’re close to a jokerless hand, holding out for real tiles can double your points, but it can also leave you waiting while someone else finishes.
  • Remember the pair rule when choosing a line. Lines full of pairs and singles get no help from jokers, so they need luckier racks.

Quick reference

  • Jokers: groups of 3 or more only (pung, kong, quint, sextet).
  • Never in singles, pairs, or single-tile parts like years and runs of singles.
  • All-joker groups are allowed when the group is 3 or more.
  • Swap a joker out of an exposed group on your turn by giving the real tile.
  • Never pass a joker. A discarded joker is generally dead.
  • Jokerless wins usually score double, except singles-and-pairs hands.
